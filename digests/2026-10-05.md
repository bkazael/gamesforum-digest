## Mario Kart Tour closes after seven years and nearly $270m in revenue
**Key Takeaway:** Analyze the long-term revenue performance of existing live-service games, especially those that have modified core monetization mechanics like gacha, to inform future game design, live ops, and sunsetting strategies.

**Metrics:** Almost seven years of service, 276 million total downloads, $268.5 million lifetime IAP revenue, Revenue peaked at $22 million in October 2019, Revenue declined to $650k/month at closure announcement, 39% ($104.7m) revenue from US, 26% ($69.8m) revenue from Japan

## Playable Ads in 2027: AI, Creative Strategy & Mobile UA
**Key Takeaway:** For mobile UA, evaluate integrating playable ads as a core strategy, explore AI tools to streamline their production while maintaining creative authenticity, and invest in analytics to iterate on specific 'Lego blocks' of playable design for better user qualification and retention.

## Discussion questions (editorial prep)
- **Given that Google has lowered Play Store commissions to around 10% (Article 5) and Unity has introduced D2C payment support (Article 5), what is the optimal balance for game operators between in-app purchases and web store D2C transactions, considering platform fees and potential player friction?**
  - [ARTICLE] The optimal balance involves evaluating the new lower platform fees against the potential for higher margins and direct customer relationships offered by D2C, while also considering player friction. Article 7 suggests D2C strategies should focus on 'additional entitlements' or exclusive content rather than just price discounts to drive conversion, as 'direct checkout' can lead to high return purchase rates (~90%). Operators must ensure D2C incentives don't destabilize the in-game economy and continually assess the regulatory environment around steering (Article 7).
- **The CPC Network is investigating major publishers over virtual currencies (Article 3), while NetEase is expanding local payment options in LATAM to address unbanked populations (Article 2). How might these two regulatory and market-driven shifts influence global monetization design for casual/puzzle, hybrid-casual, and real-money skill games?**
  - [ARTICLE] The CPC investigation (Article 3) emphasizes transparency, fair pricing, and child protection in virtual currencies within the EU, potentially requiring redesigns of monetization mechanics and pricing displays. Simultaneously, NetEase's partnership with EBANX (Article 2) highlights the growth of emerging markets through localized payment methods catering to diverse financial landscapes, including the unbanked. Operators will likely need to adopt a 'compliance by design' approach (PREVIOUS_EPISODE) for monetization, adapting to stricter EU regulations on virtual currencies while also building flexible, localized payment infrastructures to capture growth in regions like LATAM.
- **Article 8 states that only 13-15% of gaming studios use playable ads, despite significant increases in production volume for those that do. Why might this adoption rate be low, especially for the casual and hybrid-casual genres that often rely on broad UA?**
  - [ARTICLE] Article 8 indicates that the 'cost and complexity of playable production can remain a barrier' for smaller studios, despite AI lowering some of these barriers. Playables require a combination of design, art, and technical development, and assets must work across different advertising networks, which historically pushed studios towards specialist vendors. This complexity and resource requirement likely explains the lower adoption rate among the broader studio base.
- **Mario Kart Tour's revenue declined significantly after removing gacha mechanics in late 2022 (Article 6). How does this inform the balance between engaging monetization and regulatory pressure, particularly with the EU's CPC Network targeting virtual currencies (Article 3) and concerns about child protection?**
  - [ARTICLE] Mario Kart Tour's revenue decline post-gacha removal (Article 6) highlights the potential revenue impact of altering core monetization strategies. This puts pressure on operators to find alternative engaging, compliant models, especially as the EU's CPC Network (Article 3) targets virtual currencies, pushing for transparency and protecting children. The tension is between maintaining revenue streams with proven (but potentially controversial) mechanics and adhering to evolving regulatory demands that could limit 'addictive' features (PREVIOUS_EPISODE).
- **AppLovin is suing Unity over alleged data collection by its Ad Quality SDK (Article 1). Given this ongoing legal battle and the reported $1B funding for AppsFlyer focusing on AI-powered advertising analytics (Article 5), what are the implications for mobile UA and ad quality in a privacy-conscious, data-driven environment?**
  - [ARTICLE] The legal dispute between AppLovin and Unity (Article 1) underscores increasing scrutiny over ad tech data collection and usage, creating uncertainty for advertisers. Simultaneously, AppsFlyer's significant funding into AI-powered analytics (Article 5) suggests continued investment in sophisticated measurement tools despite privacy changes. For mobile UA (Article 8), this implies a critical need for transparency and compliance in data practices, a reliance on advanced, potentially AI-driven, attribution and ad quality tools, and careful vendor selection to mitigate legal and privacy risks while striving for effective, authentic ad experiences.
- **Minecraft Blast is re-entering soft launch (Article 4) and Mario Kart Tour has been shut down (Article 6), both major IP titles. What does this suggest about the lifecycle management of games leveraging strong IP in the mobile market, especially for puzzle and casual genres?**
  - [ARTICLE] The re-soft launch of Minecraft Blast (Article 4) suggests that even with strong IP, a game needs extensive testing and iteration to find a successful market fit, especially if initial attempts fall short. Conversely, the shutdown of Mario Kart Tour after seven years (Article 6), despite significant revenue, indicates that even successful IP titles require continuous engagement (PREVIOUS_EPISODE) and evolving monetization strategies. The removal of gacha mechanics (Article 6) further highlights the challenges in balancing revenue generation with player experience and regulatory changes, implying that IP alone is not a guarantee of perpetual success or immunity from market shifts.
